## 1. What this is

A personal website with two modes behind one door: **Work mode** (founders, hiring managers, recruiters) and **Know Me mode** (everyone who should remember him). Built AI-native: planned in chat, built by Pragaman via Claude Code — itself a proof of method.

**Positioning (confirmed):** "I care about what I build, not what it's called." Purpose- and problem-driven, not title-driven.

**Hero line candidate (from the wedding test):** *"I solve problems. I save time. I make brands famous."*

## 2. Success criteria

- **Work mode exit feeling:** "This person ships, and the numbers are real. I should talk to him." *(Draft — Pragaman to confirm — Open item 3)*
- **Know Me exit feeling (confirmed):** "I met someone interesting." Visitor remembers 1–2 specific things and laughed at least once.
- **The conversion:** email · Google Calendar booking · The Intake. No "hire me" banners anywhere — evergreen site.

## 3. Architecture (all locked)

- **Landing:** full-screen two doors + one witty framing line.
- **Mode state:** global; chosen at the door; flippable anywhere via persistent toggle (headshot in Work / memoji in Know Me — permanent); remembered across visits; deep-linkable.
- **Mode transition:** animated temperature shift — warm-white → cream, accent set expands. Felt, not instant.
- **Sound:** on by default, subtle, Comeau-calibrated, visible mute. Library `use-sound`. Moments: toggle flip, easter egg discovery, Intake submit. Never on scroll.
- **Design foundations:** `pragaman-design-foundations-v1.md` — Bricolage Grotesque / Satoshi / Space Mono / Caveat · The Coral Thread palette. Space Mono wears every number, no exceptions.
- **Stack:** Next.js + Tailwind + Vercel (inherited from Sapiens, now company-standard).

### 4.2 Case Study 1 — Filing Buddy (growth)

**Template:** 30-second layer on top (problem → 4 moves → results strip), full story below. **Framing:** "The year before me vs. my first year" (before = Jul '24–Jun '25, pre-Pragaman; after = Jul '25–Jul '26 = his internship year). Emphasize: done as an intern.

#### Narrative beats (sourced W13–W18 + report)
1. **The before-picture** (report): avg position 45 = page 5, invisible. 2,500 organic clicks in a *year*. Traffic 61.3% paid — the site rented its audience. 🔴 *Open: what did the client believe was possible? (W13a-2)*
2. **The four moves** (W14, structured for the 30-second layer):
   - **Depth over breadth** — researched and ranked target keywords, identified content pillars, built content depth instead of spreading thin.
   - **The publishing engine** — standardized the entire blog-writing process; regular blog + glossary cadence.
   - **Universal search optimization** — one content strategy pushed across Google, social platforms, and AI answers. *(Rendered spelled-out, not as a branded acronym, unless Pragaman claims coinage.)*
   - **The technical overhaul** — fixed site technical issues, raised audit score, attacked loading speed.
3. **The doubt period** (W15): early months — content quality was there, traffic wasn't. Diagnosis: technical debt and slow loading were suffocating good content. Fixed tech, improved speed, added user-friendly features. 🔴 *Open: how many months flat before the turn? (W15a)*
4. **The turn** (W16): **January 2026** — monthly run-rate crossed **1M impressions** and ~6K monthly clicks. Signal confirmed → scaled.
5. **The AEO playbook** (W17): wrote in LLM-friendly language and structure ("LLM bait"), modified robots.txt, E-E-A-T content, YMYL standards, PAA + FAQ integration, explained with examples and illustrations — before this was a mainstream playbook. Receipt: **213K AI Overview impressions since May '26**.
6. **The client reaction** (W18): shocked that AI engines cite their blogs in answers; delighted at 1M+ organic impressions/month. *(Paraphrase — no verbatim quote available yet; W38 may supply one later.)*

**The muscle this proves:** 0→1 product work — requirements, stakeholders, PRD, delivery — vs. Filing Buddy's growth muscle.

#### Narrative beats (sourced W19, W20, W22 + W5 anchor)
1. **The anchor scene** (W5): first month as an intern, sent alone to the client's office in Indore. A week of stakeholder interviews across GST, Taxation, Accounting, Finance, Compliance, Sales. Output: the PRD. Then coordinated dev, co-designed UI/UX, ran ops↔dev logic and data-flow alignment through delivery.
2. **The before-picture** (W19): every department running its own Excel sheet *and* updating a shared master sheet. Client follow-ups manual, one by one, across email threads and WhatsApp. Deadlines and challans tracked by hand. A 2-minute task took 10. One absence stalled a whole chain. → The software collapsed all of it.
3. **The stakeholder circus** (W20): 20+ people; some treated the software as a magic wand — full automation, zero willingness to touch it. Departments available one day, gone the next; requirements gathered part in-person, part online; conflicting inputs synchronized into flows a dev team could actually build from.

### 4.4 Case Study 3 — Sapiens.club (the solo build) — ✅ narrative complete

**One-liner (from the live site, adopted):** *Sapiens — the anti-social-network. Real people helping real people nearby: no money, no profiles, no feeds. Launching in India, 2026.*

#### Narrative beats (W24–W28, Pragaman's own drafts — approved voice, light editing only)
1. **Origin** (W24): the idea came from **"my boss"** *(name withheld — locked)* — one sentence, no spec, no team, no roadmap. "The idea, him. Everything after the word 'idea' — me." Scoped the MVP, chose the stack (**Next.js, Supabase, Sanity, Vercel**), built the entire thing with AI as the only teammate. "When there's a bug in production, there's exactly one person to blame, and unfortunately I know him personally."
2. **Why alone:** part circumstance, part stubbornness — proving one person with product judgment and AI leverage can ship what used to take a team. "I stopped thinking of 'I can't build that' as a valid sentence."
3. **The wall** (W25): not the code — everything around it. **The D-U-N-S number story** (midnight paperwork archaeology). Pattern: unknown unknowns — certificates, signing, store requirements. Method: tight loops with AI — hit wall → ask → understand → verify against real docs → implement. "AI can answer any question, but when you're solo, figuring out which questions exist is entirely your job."
4. **The reverse 2 AM story** (W26): lost his sense of time in flow, "one quick thing before office" became 11:30 AM. "My body was at home, my brain was somewhere inside the codebase." Fix: "a very sophisticated engineering solution — alarms."
5. **AI-development lessons** (W27): the unlock is **range, not speed**; AI **relocates the struggle** — locally correct, globally incoherent code means the job becomes decisions and coherence-enforcement; "AI amplifies whoever has the clearest thinking; it doesn't substitute for it." Still runs Sapiens through Claude Code: "I direct, it executes, I review."
6. **State** (W28): MVP complete — idea → website → PRD → working mobile app for **both Apple and Android**, shipped solo; now in testing ("every bug I catch now is a one-star review that never gets written"). Dream: real users on both stores telling us something unexpected — "an MVP is a hypothesis."
7. **Flagship proof point:** the stack he chose solo was **adopted company-wide** for other client sites.

### 4.5 How I Work — **The Bedrock Method** ✅ named

- **The method, rendered as a diagram (not a paragraph):** generate every possible question → group them → imagine the flow → answer from most technical down to most basic → drill until you hit bedrock — nothing left unexamined, foundation solid — then build up.
- **Diagram concept for design spec:** drilling down through question strata to bedrock, building the product on top. (First-2-weeks texture from W36 available for copy: questions on the glass wall, marker, relevance boxes, phased chunks → PRD → MVP → feedback.)
- **The 3–4× automation rule (verbatim):** "If I do something 3–4 times, I build a system for it." Live example: the prospect engine — self-built lead-finder qualifying companies by condition, populating outreach lists; next step, automating personalized first contact.
- **Honest edges (self-awareness, not sanded off):** obsessive about completion, finishes early, cold when standards slip, actively working on patience + listening without interrupting.
- **Range at Noboru** (W3): client handling, brand strategy, software PRDs, UI/UX, vendor management, marketing & sales funnels, PR, social, video shoots, technical SEO, design-to-deploy websites; managing people before graduating. *(Candidate title from W40, placement TBD: "The Everything Guy.")*
